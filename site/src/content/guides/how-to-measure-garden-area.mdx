  - q: How do I convert square metres to square feet?
    a: Multiply square metres by 10.764. So 50 m² is about 538 ft². To go the other way, divide square feet by 10.764.
  - q: How do I measure a sloping garden?
    a: For turf, bark or membrane, measure along the surface of the slope, because that's the area you'll cover. For fencing and planning purposes, measure horizontally.

## What you need

- A long tape measure (a 30 m surveyor's tape is easiest), or a laser measure
- A pen and paper to sketch the garden
- Someone to hold the other end

## Measuring a rectangular garden

1. Measure the length from the back of the house to the far boundary.
2. Measure the width from one side boundary to the other.
3. Multiply them: **length × width = area**.

## Measuring an awkward shape

Most gardens aren't perfect rectangles. Sketch the garden from above and split it into simple shapes:

| Shape | What to measure | Area |
|---|---|---|
| Rectangle | Length and width | length × width |
| Triangle | Base and height (at right angles to the base) | base × height ÷ 2 |
| Circle | Diameter (straight across the middle) | 3.14 × (diameter ÷ 2)² |

Work out each shape and add them up. For a flower bed, shed or patio you want to leave out, work it out the same way and subtract it.

## Worked example

An L-shaped garden can be split into two rectangles: 10 m × 6 m (60 m²) and 4 m × 3 m (12 m²). Take off a 3 m × 2 m shed (6 m²):

60 + 12 − 6 = **66 m²**, which is about **710 ft²**.

<Callout type="tip">
  Measure in metres if you can. Turf, gravel, bark and paving are all sold by the square metre in the UK, so you won't need to convert.
</Callout>
