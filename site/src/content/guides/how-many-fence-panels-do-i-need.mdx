faqs:
  - q: What size is a standard fence panel?
    a: Most UK garden fence panels are 6 ft wide (1.83 m), and commonly 3 ft to 6 ft high. Shorter widths are available for awkward gaps.
  - q: Should I use concrete or timber posts?
    a: Concrete posts with concrete gravel boards last longer and won't rot, but are heavier and look more industrial. Pressure-treated timber posts look better with timber panels and are easier to cut, but have a shorter life in wet ground.
  - q: How deep should fence posts go?
    a: As a rule of thumb, about a quarter of the post length goes in the ground, so a 2.4 m post for a 1.8 m fence sits about 600 mm deep.

## How the numbers work

1. **Measure the full length** of the fence line in metres. Measure along the ground where the posts will go, not across the top of a slope.
2. **Divide by the panel width** and round up. A part panel still needs a whole panel, which is cut to fit.
3. **Add the posts.** A straight run has one post between each pair of panels plus one at each end, so it's always one more post than panels. If the fence is in two separate runs, such as both sides of a garden, add one extra end post per run.
4. **One gravel board per panel.** Gravel boards sit under the panel and stop it touching the soil, so they take the rot instead of the panel.
5. **Post mix.** Allow about two bags of quick-setting post mix per post. Bigger or deeper holes take more.

## Worked example

A back garden with 12 m down one side and 8 m across the back, joined at the corner, is one run of 20 m.

- 20 ÷ 1.83 = 10.9, so **11 panels**
- 11 panels + 1 = **12 posts** (the corner post is shared)
- **11 gravel boards**
- 12 posts × 2 = about **24 bags** of post mix

## Closeboard fencing is different

Closeboard (featheredge) fences aren't built from panels. They use posts every 2.4 to 3 m, two or three horizontal rails between posts, and vertical featheredge boards overlapping along the rails. They're stronger than panel fences, which is why they're often used on exposed sites and long runs.
