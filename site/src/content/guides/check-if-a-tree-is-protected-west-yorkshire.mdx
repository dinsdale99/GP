## Three ways a tree can be protected

1. **Tree Preservation Order (TPO).** The council has made an order on a specific tree, group or woodland.
2. **Conservation area.** Trees in a conservation area with a trunk more than 7.5 cm across, measured 1.5 m above the ground, are protected even without a TPO.
3. **Planning condition.** When planning permission is granted, the council can require existing trees to be kept. This shows up on the planning decision for the site.

## How to check in each council

| Council | Covers | How to check |
|---|---|---|
| Leeds City Council | Leeds, Otley, Wetherby, Morley, Pudsey | Leeds has an online protected trees map you can search by address or postcode ([council announcement](https://news.leeds.gov.uk/news/log-on-and-twig-protected-tree-locations-in-leeds-as-council-branches-out-with-new-online-mapping-service)). Order documents are published at tpo.leeds.gov.uk. |
| Bradford Council | Bradford, Keighley, Shipley, Ilkley, Bingley | Use Bradford's [find out if a tree is protected](https://www.bradford.gov.uk/planning-and-building-control/trees-and-conservation/trees-protected-by-tree-preservation-orders/) page and its online map, or call the planning service. |
| Wakefield Council | Wakefield, Pontefract, Castleford, Ossett | TPOs are shown on the council's public map and on "Where I Live". See [Wakefield tree preservation orders](https://www.wakefield.gov.uk/planning/heritage-conservation-and-tree-preservation/tree-preservation-orders/). |
| Kirklees Council | Huddersfield, Dewsbury, Batley, Holmfirth | Kirklees publishes its [TPO area data](https://ckan.publishing.service.gov.uk/organization/kirklees-council?tags=TPO). Contact the council's planning service to confirm a specific tree. |
| Calderdale Council | Halifax, Brighouse, Hebden Bridge, Todmorden | Calderdale publishes a [TPO dataset](https://dataworks.calderdale.gov.uk/dataset/tree-preservation-orders-233pj). Contact the council's planning service to confirm a specific tree. |

<Callout type="tip">
  Not sure which council you're in? Your council tax bill shows it. Towns near the boundaries, like Morley or Brighouse, are easy to get wrong.
</Callout>

## If the tree is protected

- **TPO:** apply to the council for consent before any work. Applications are free and usually decided within eight weeks.
- **Conservation area (no TPO):** give the council six weeks' written notice of the work. They can then either let it go ahead or make a TPO.
- **Planning condition:** check the wording of the condition and speak to the council planning officer.
